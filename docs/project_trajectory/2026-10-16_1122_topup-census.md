## 2026-10-16 11:22 — How many top-ups the figure sweeps need under the 1.05x read-out

Pavel chose adaptive training: train at a first-guess budget, then warm-start a top-up for any run
that has not trained long enough past its read-out. He also chose to keep a snapshot every 1,000
iterations. This entry defines a run's status and counts the top-ups.

**Status of a run** (read with `common.noisy_floor` at b121e02, on its full warm-start chain):
- **done:** it has a 1.05x read-out t*, it trained to at least 2x t*, and its loss does not end
  below 0.9x its floor.
- **top-up:** it is short of 2x t* (target 3x t*); or its loss ends below 0.9x its floor (target 2x
  its current length, then re-read); or it has not yet stayed under the bar (target 3x the crossing
  the fitted curve predicts, if that is within 10x its length, else 2x its length). After each
  top-up the run is read again, and topped up again if it is still not done.
- **unreadable:** a top-up does not help. The reasons are a fit pinned at its bound, a loss ending
  more than 1.5x above its floor, bursts over half of training, or a loss that never leaves its
  starting level.

**Scope.** Every sweep that a paper, supplementary or COSYNE figure script names (`fig_paper_*`,
`fig_supp_*`, `fig_cosyne2027`, `b5_read`, `ff75k_readout`, `fig_knobs_at_floor`), plus their
continuation sweeps (`_c75000`, `_c200000`). Della sweeps were read on Della (job 15690495); the
Spock-era sweeps were read from the Mac copies, which are the only copies left. Only the last run of
each warm-start chain is classified: 1,149 runs. Tables: `data/readout_status_{mac,della}.csv`
(not committed). Scripts `readout_status.py` and `status_summary.py` are in the session
scratchpad.

**Result.**
- 873 runs (76%) are done, 151 (13%) need a top-up and 125 (11%) are unreadable.
- Top-ups in the sweeps behind the paper figures (paper grids, B5 optimizer, DMTS, the bias-0 CDDM
  sweeps, the snapshot sweeps, ff_revive): 87 runs, 13.7 M extra iterations, about 1,760 GPU-hours.
  - The largest items are the flip-flop paper grid at 75k (9 runs, about 540 GPU-h, two of them at
    N=4000) and DMTS_d7_pen (19 runs, about 580 GPU-h).
- The other figure sweeps: 64 runs, 21.4 M iterations, about 6,070 GPU-hours.
  - Dominated by NBitFlipFlop_std_bigN at N=4000 (17 runs, about 3,570 GPU-h; one run alone about
    890 GPU-h) and std_penlong (13 runs, about 1,580 GPU-h).
- Median top-up: the run doubles its length (90th percentile 3.3x). A second round is expected for
  some runs, since the floor is refitted on the longer series.
- GPU-hours use the guide's flip-flop speeds (N=500 0.18, 1000 0.27, 2000 0.7, 4000 2.4
  s/iteration) and assume CDDM runs at 0.3x those. These are estimates.

**Unreadable, and not fixable by training** (125 runs):
- 53 never leave their starting level. (Corrected the same day, after plotting every trajectory: an
  earlier version of this entry said the DMTS arms start from a trained network. That was a guess,
  and it was wrong.)
  - 14 are warm-started runs: the `std_switch` sweeps, CDDM and flip-flop. They begin near their
    floor, so the escape test finds no escape. That case needs a rule decision.
  - 33 are DMTS paper-grid runs, trained from scratch, that never learned. They are 18 in the mute
    arm (dropout on), 13 in the duplication arm and 2 controls, and they sit at the constant-output
    level, about 0.063, for all 150k iterations. DMTS stalls are known to last up to 155k before an
    escape, so a top-up might rescue some; "unreadable" is the wrong label for them.
  - 6 more stall the same way: 3 in std_penlong (k=6-7, frm), 2 in DMTS_d7_pen, 1 in B5 SGD.
- 55 have a loss ending more than 1.5x above its floor: 20 in the older CDDM_std_g0, and DMTS_d7_pen
  at N=2000.
- 16 have a fit pinned at its lower bound.
