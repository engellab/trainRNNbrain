## 2026-11-08 09:43 — Why met0.01 seed 1 froze: post-episode states overflow the metabolic penalty; the fix, the affected runs, and how many guard restores this explains

Corrects the reading of met0.01 seed 1 in `2026-11-08_0915_round19-results.md`. It was "not learned"; it is
**"frozen by a non-finite loss"**. Tracking: ptolmachev/dead-relu-paper#201.

**Terms.**
- *Post-episode step*: an RNN step after the agent has reached the cheese. The trainer keeps stepping the RNN
  on the last observation until the episode's T = 500 decisions are over, in the rollout and in the PPO
  recompute (the second forward pass, with the stored noise, through which the loss is differentiated).
- *Running step*: an RNN step before the end.
- *Guard restore*: the rollback guard discards a rollout whose log-probabilities or rewards are not finite and
  restores the last finite weights.
- *Guard stop*: six such failures in a row end the run.

### The skipped-step scan

I copied all 373 Maze Progress records on both clusters and scanned them with
`round19_postepisode.py skipped`. That covers every Maze tree: the suite, the interventions, the pilot, the calibration and
the retrain runs. A skipped step is an optimiser step whose loss was not finite. Consecutive steps were counted
across updates.
- Only met0.01 seed 1 has a run of 100 or more consecutive skipped steps.
- In stage 1 its longest run is 3,200 steps, from update 1,601, with 4,001 skipped in total.
- In stage 2 it skipped 15,999 of 16,000 steps, with a longest run of 9,415.
- The next-longest run in any record is 7 steps.

### The cause

`met_diag.py`, `postep.py`, `met_fix_check.py` and `replay.py`, named below, are one-off diagnostics kept
in my scratch notes, not in the repository; each rebuilds a network with `TrainerPPO.load_run` and plays
training rollouts.

I rebuilt the network from its stage-2 folder, whose weights have not changed since update 1,500. I played one
training rollout of 16 episodes and evaluated each loss term as `TrainerPPO.ppo_update` does (`met_diag.py`):
- Every running step has |x| ≤ 1.8.
- In one of the 16 episodes, the states after the episode ended grow to |x| ≈ 1e21, starting about 1,500 RNN
  steps into the episode.
- The metabolic penalty is the mean squared rate over every step, post-episode steps included. The square
  overflows float32 (largest value 3.4e38) to inf, so the loss is inf and the minibatch is skipped.
- The policy loss is finite.

The module docstring says every loss is a mean over running steps only. The policy loss and the value loss
are, through the alive mask, but the rate penalty and the metabolic penalty are not. **This is a code defect.**
It also reaches the policy loss in another way: a non-finite value at a post-episode step survives the alive
mask, because multiplying a non-finite value by 0 gives NaN. The minibatch is then skipped, or, when the
rollout itself is non-finite, the guard restores.

**The fix** is `trainer.freeze_after_end` (commit d2d0b3f). When it is on:
- an ended episode's state is held at its end value, in the rollout and in the recompute alike, so the PPO
  ratio still starts at 1;
- the two penalties average over running steps only.

Tests: `test_freeze_after_end_holds_ended_episodes` and `test_penalties_over_live_steps`; all 38 maze tests pass.

Checked on the frozen network (`met_fix_check.py`, 4 rollouts of 32 episodes):

| freeze_after_end | Rollouts with a non-finite metabolic penalty | Largest \|x\| |
|---|---|---|
| off | 4 of 4 | 5.75e35 |
| on | 0 of 4 | 2.5 |

**The default stays false.** Every existing run used false, and the coordinator decides the default after
this entry. Since commit e8ae647 the trainer also records, for each discarded rollout, whether it was
non-finite after an episode's end only or within a running episode. The kind is printed in the log and saved
as `restored_post_episode` in the Progress record.

### Read-outs: running steps only (checked in the code)

- **The policy loss and the value loss:** `ppo_update` multiplies by `m = alive[:, idx]` and divides by
  `m.sum()`. The advantages are normalised over alive steps.
- **The participation read-out of training (`TrainerPPO.probe`):** a clean rollout. Participation is taken
  over `alive[:-1]` repeated `substeps` times, so only running steps count.
- **Every analysis read-out of active units on the Maze:** `rl_maze_ablate.probe_participation` (used by
  `rl_maze_retrain.py`), `round18_maze.py`, `rl_maze_population_stats.py` and `round19_maze_inspect.py`. Each
  masks with the same alive rows. Every figure's Maze active count comes from the probe's saved `active`
  array. **No active-unit read-out includes post-episode steps.**
- **What does use post-episode steps,** apart from the two penalties: the per-unit rules' training-time
  statistics. These are the rescaling and duplication checks (`prune_and_reinit` on the recompute's states),
  the rescale graduation (`frm_activity`) and the read-out dropout's participation average (from the rollout's
  states). They are inputs to training, not read-outs. `freeze_after_end` does not change them; with it on they
  see the held state. Whether they should be restricted to running steps is open, for the coordinator.

### How many guard restores and stops this explains

**What can and cannot be measured.** A restore happens at weights that are never saved: the last finite
weights before the failing update. The run folder keeps only the latest checkpoint, and kept snapshots exist
only for ReLU N = 1,000 seeds 4-6 in stage 1, every 100 updates. So a past restore can be classified only
where weights from close to it exist.

**The stops whose last saved weights are finite** (`postep.py`, 64 training episodes each):

| Network | Stopped at update | \|x\| in running steps | \|x\| after the end | Class |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rwm0.2 s1, stage 2 | 3,001 | ≤ 1.27 | 6.2e37 (1 episode of 64) | post-episode growth |
| met0.01 s1 (frozen, not stopped) | (skipped steps from 1,501) | ≤ 1.9 | 2.3e21 (9 of 64) | post-episode growth |
| rescale_mild s1, stage 1 | 800 | inf (63 of 64 episodes) | inf | growth in running episodes |
| rescale_tgt4 s2, stage 1 | 800 | inf (63 of 64) | inf | growth in running episodes |

**The other nine stopped networks can't be classified.** Their last saved weights are themselves non-finite:
rwm0.2 s3, rescale_few s2 and s3, inorm20 s1 (stage 2), inorm5 s1-s3 and inorm20 s2-s3 (stage 1). The inorm5
and inorm20 runs had every unit inactive at their last probe, and they stopped within 200-700 updates of
starting, which points to running-episode growth under a large input norm; their weights cannot confirm it.

**End weights of every other network** (`round19_postepisode.py growth`, output
`data/round19/postepisode_growth.jsonl`, 32 training episodes each, 155 networks with
finite weights at N ≤ 2,000):
- Post-episode states above 1e6 occur in 3 networks: met0.01 s1, rwm0.2 s1 and rescale_mild s1.
- Running-episode states above 1e6 occur in 2: the two rescaling stops.
- The median largest |x| is 2.4 in running steps and 1.5 after the end; the 95th percentiles are 7.4 and 5.3.
- Of the 44 networks with at least one restore, 1 shows post-episode growth at its end weights and 2 show
  running growth. Of the 111 without a restore, 2 show post-episode growth.

This test has little power. A restore occurs at roughly 1 to 3 updates in 1,000, at weights different from
the end weights, so 32 episodes at the end weights rarely catch the condition that caused it. A replay of
4 × 256 episodes at the snapshot 41 updates before a restore of ReLU seed 4 (update 1,041) found no non-finite
value anywhere. That is inconclusive for the same reason.

**N = 2,000 and 4,000:**
- N = 2,000: at their end weights none of the three networks has growth in either part.
- N = 4,000 was not read. CPU is too slow at that size, and end weights are weak evidence anyway.
- Their restores are unclassified. N = 4,000 restores per stage, stages 1-3: seed 1 0, 18 and 43; seed 2 0, 11 and 0; seed 3 0, 41 and 41. Stages 1-2: seed 4 1 and 1; seed 5 0 and 8.

**Answer.** Of the guard events that can be classified, the stage-2 stop of rwm0.2 s1 and the freeze of
met0.01 s1 come from post-episode growth. The two stage-1 rescaling stops come from growth inside running
episodes. **The data do not say how many of the other restores, including the stage-2 excess (24 of 63
networks against 12 of 63 in stage 1), are post-episode ones.** Measuring that needs runs with the new
classification, listed under the options.

**How the Adam-restart test (Spock 7402515) should be read.** That test ran on code before e8ae647, without
the classification, and with `freeze_after_end` off.
- *If the INIT_ADAM=true reruns have no restore:* the restart raised the rate of non-finite rollouts. For
  rwm0.2 s1, at least, those were post-episode blow-ups. `freeze_after_end=true` removes post-episode
  blow-ups whatever the optimiser state. So, read alone, that result would support `INIT_ADAM=true` only for
  runs that keep `freeze_after_end` off.
- *If they do have restores:* the restart is not the cause.
- *Either way,* the test does not tell restart-induced from post-episode instability. To make it informative
  for the protocol, repeat it with the classification on (option A below).

### Affected runs

1. **Lost every optimiser step to the overflow:** met0.01 s1, stage 1 from update 1,501 and all of stage 2.
   Reported as "frozen by a non-finite loss".
2. **Stopped by post-episode growth:** rwm0.2 s1, stage 2. Reported as "stopped by the guard, post-episode
   growth".
3. **Penalty averaged over post-episode steps (every run with lambda_frm > 0 or lambda_met > 0):**
   frm0.03, frm0.1, frm0.3, frm0.1_rws0.05, met0.01, met0.1, met1 and met10, seeds 1-3 each (24 networks, and
   their stage-2 continuations). Post-episode steps were 20-56% of RNN steps over the last 200 updates.
   - Their verdicts are withdrawn.
   - The 24 are being rerun with `freeze_after_end=true`, Della 17107385, as decided
     (`2026-11-08_0940_round19-penalty-reruns-rules.md`).
4. **Per-unit rules whose statistics include post-episode steps:** dropout0.2, dropout0.05, rescale, rescale_mild,
   rescale_few, rescale_tgt4, duplicate, redraw and zeroout. They are not defective in the sense above, since
   no read-out is affected, but they act on post-episode activity. Open, listed for the coordinator.
5. **Other restores and stops:** unclassified, as above.

### Options for the default (the coordinator's decision), with costs

- **A. Measure first:** about 18 GPU-h. Run the Adam-test networks (rwm0.2 s1, srec0.1 s3, srad1.5 s3) once more
  in stage 2, with `INIT_ADAM=false` and `freeze_after_end` off, on the current code. These are same-condition
  replicates, which the Adam test lacks, and every restore they have is classified post-episode or running.
  For about 70 GPU-h, add 9 more networks with stage-2 restores, to put a number on the stage-2 excess.
- **B. `true` for every new Maze run; existing chains continue with `false`.** No extra cost. In a run with no
  penalty and no per-unit rule, the setting changes nothing but whether post-episode values can become
  non-finite: the gradients are the same, and read-outs never use post-episode steps. Penalty arms are
  already being rerun. The per-unit rules would see held states, which is a change for those arms.
- **C. B, and also switch existing chains at their next stage.** No extra cost, but a chain's stages would then
  differ in the setting. The launcher now refuses that mismatch, so allowing it would be an explicit change.
- **D. Rerun every existing Maze network with `true`.** About 170 networks × 5.5 GPU-h per stage, roughly
  950 GPU-h per stage. Not recommended: for networks with no penalty and no rule the setting changes only
  finiteness.

**My recommendation: B, with A to measure** how much of the stage-2 instability `true` removes. Also, rerun
rwm0.2 s1's stage 2 with `true` (about 6 GPU-h) to test whether it then completes.
