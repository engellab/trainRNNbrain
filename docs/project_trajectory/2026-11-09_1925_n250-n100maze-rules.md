## 2026-11-09 19:25 — Size series: N = 250 networks on 3BFF and CDDM, N = 100 networks on Maze (inventory and rules, before any job runs)

**Why.** Pavel, 9 Nov: "can you also train 250 unit networks: CDDM and 3BFF, to complete the picture. You can also
try to train 100 unit maze networks." The 3BFF and CDDM size series of Figure 2 and Supplementary Table 1 has
networks of N = 100, 500, 1,000, 2,000 and 4,000 units; the Maze series has N = 250 to 4,000. This entry records
what the existing series are, fixes what is trained, how it is read and how the new sizes enter the fits. No job
of this package had started when it was written.

**Terms.** *3BFF*: the 3-bit flip-flop task. *CDDM*: context-dependent decision making. *Maze*: the egocentric
maze task trained by PPO (proximal policy optimisation, a reinforcement-learning algorithm). *Floor crossing*:
the first iteration t after which the noisy training loss, smoothed, stays at or below 1.05 times its fitted
floor through 1.2·t (`common.read_run`; `docs/AGENT_GUIDE.md`, "Reading networks"). *Active unit*: participation
p = std(r) + q0.9(|r|) on a noise-free pass, active if p ≥ 0.05·q95(p). *Update* (Maze): one batch of 256
episodes followed by 8 optimiser steps. *Stage* (Maze): 2,000 updates. *Learned point* (Maze,
`common.maze_learned_index`): the first probe from which the noise-free probe success stays at or above 0.9 for
five probes in a row, in a record whose last probe is also at or above 0.9.

### Training protocol for the Maze networks

As in entry 2026-11-07_1008: every network of one size shares one budget. Stages 1 and 2 (updates 1-2,000 and
2,001-4,000) are trained for all four networks, because 4,000 updates is the budget of every size from 250 to
2,000. Each continuation uses the suite launcher's continuation mode with `INIT_ADAM=false` (Adam starts afresh
and the 50-update warm-up runs again), as every continuation of the series did. At update 4,000,
`common.maze_learned_index` is applied to each joined record. If all four networks are learned, training stops.
If any is not, all four train another stage, and so on to the cap of 8,000 updates. A network not learned at
8,000 stops there.

### Read-outs (the paper's own)

**3BFF and CDDM** (`fig_paper_F2_size.read_one`, the function `collect()` applies to every series network):
- the floor crossing at 1.05× the fitted floor of the noisy training loss, and the 1.10× and 1.20× crossings;
- the number of active units at the participation probe nearest each crossing (noise-free trace);
- held-out R² under the training noise at the end and at the 1.05× crossing (`heldout_r2`, 36 noise draws,
  CPU), measured on a kept snapshot if one exists near the crossing, else estimated as in the guide;
- a network without a crossing is excluded from every comparison at the floor and counted, with its reason.

**Maze** (`fig_paper_F2_size.maze_series`, as `round19_size_exponent.py` reads it): the learned point; the number
of active units at the learned point, at update 4,000 and at the end of training; probe success at each.

**Script.** One read script for this package, `trainRNNbrain/experiments_and_analysis/size250_n100maze_read.py`,
written after the jobs end. It calls `read_one` on the six new supervised networks (on Della, through `sbatch`),
takes every other size from the collect record of Figure 2, and fits with `power_fit` and `boot_exponent`
(`BOOT_SEED`, 2,000 resamples within each size). For Maze it calls `maze_series` and the same fit functions. As
a check it first reproduces the values stated above (0.478, 0.443; 0.343, 0.471).

### How the new sizes enter the fits (decided now)

- **The primary fits stay as they are.** 3BFF and CDDM: N ≥ 500, at 1.05× (0.478 and 0.443), also at 1.10× and
  1.20×. Maze: N ≥ 500 at the learned point (0.343), at update 4,000 (0.495) and at the end (0.441). The range
  N ≥ 500 was fixed before the first fit (entry 2026-10-31_1900) and does not move.
- **The new sizes are drawn and tabulated:** each new network gets a row in Supplementary Table 1 (3BFF, CDDM)
  or in the Maze table of Extended Data, and a point in Fig. 2a,b, drawn but outside the primary fit, as N = 100
  is today.
- **The every-size fits gain the new sizes,** and both versions are reported. 3BFF and CDDM: old over
  N = 100, 500, 1,000, 2,000, 4,000; new with N = 250 added; at 1.05×, 1.10× and 1.20×. Maze: old over
  N = 250 to 4,000; new with N = 100 added; at the learned point, at update 4,000 (networks learned by then) and
  at the final budgets (unequal budgets, labelled so).
- A network that never learns (Maze) or has no floor crossing (3BFF, CDDM) enters no mean and no fit, and is
  listed.

### What is expected, and what would be surprising

Expected: on 3BFF about 140 of 250 units active (the N ≥ 500 power law gives 145 at N = 250, interpolation on
log axes between the N = 100 and N = 500 means gives 138); on CDDM about 180-220 (interpolation 178, N ≥ 500
law 224); held-out R² at the crossing near the other sizes' (3BFF 0.95, CDDM 0.83). On Maze, the every-size law
extrapolates to 117 active units at N = 100, more than the 100 units available, so nearly every unit should be
active at N = 100, as on the supervised tasks at N = 100 (82 and 90 of 100).

Each of these would be surprising, and each is reported in the results entry and on the status board if it
occurs:

1. a mean number of active units at N = 250 (3BFF, CDDM) below the N = 100 mean or above the N = 500 mean: the
   number of active units would not grow with N between 100 and 500;
2. a CDDM network at N = 250 with half of its units or fewer active (125 or fewer); on 3BFF the expected count,
   about 140, is close to half, so the 3BFF count is reported without that label;
3. an N = 250 network with no floor crossing within its budget;
4. held-out R² at the crossing more than 0.01 below every N = 100 and N = 500 network of the same task;
5. a Maze network at N = 100 not learned within 8,000 updates (reported as not learned, with its success);
6. a mean active count at the Maze learned point at N = 100 above the N = 250 mean (150);
7. a new every-size exponent outside the bootstrap interval of the old one.

None of these changes the primary fits (above).
